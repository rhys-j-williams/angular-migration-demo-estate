Saved 5 September 2026. Read this whole document before writing any code. Then create a CLAUDE.md in the workspace root that summarises the rules in section 2 and the phase you are in, and keep it updated as you go.

## 1. What this is and who will read it

You are building a fictional bank's software estate for a live sales demonstration of Devin, Cognition's autonomous software engineer. The demo shows Devin planning and executing an Angular 14 to Angular 18 upgrade (and then onward to 22) across a shared component library and the applications that consume it, inside an environment that looks and behaves like a large US retail bank's real one. The estate will be indexed by Devin, read by Devin, and changed by Devin in pull requests. It will also be looked at by a bank's VP of Engineering, a Security Engineer and a Chief Architect, who will recognise their own world if you get it right and will lose interest if it looks like a tutorial.

The estate is modelled on a large US bank's publicly evidenced technology, drawn from its job postings and executive interviews. Nothing in the estate may use that bank's real names, product names, logos, domains or copy. The fictional bank is Meridian Trust Bank, and the naming map in section 4 is mandatory.

The three qualities that matter, in order. Realism, meaning the code, configuration, history and documentation look like they were produced by many teams over five years. Deliberate breakage, meaning a catalogue of specific problems an Angular 14 estate accumulates, placed exactly where a bank would have them, so that Devin has something real to find. Buildability, meaning every repository builds and its tests pass at its current versions, from a clean checkout, using only the local mocks described here.

## 2. Rules that override everything else

R1. Do not upgrade anything. Every repository stays at the versions in section 5. If a dependency at the stated version cannot be installed, use the nearest version inside the same major and record the substitution in BUILD_LOG.md. Never move an Angular major, a Material major, a TypeScript major or a Node major.

R2. Do not fix the traps. Section 8 lists deliberate problems. They must be present, they must be findable, and the code around them must still compile and pass tests at the current version. If a trap would stop the build at the current version, it is placed wrong. Re-read the trap and put it somewhere it bites only at the upgrade step named for it.

R3. Everything external is mocked and runs locally. No calls to real banks, real identity providers, real message brokers in the cloud, real package registries other than the public npm and Maven Central for bootstrapping, or real vendors. Section 7 lists the mocks. Where Docker is available, use compose. Where it is not, every mock must also have an in process fallback.

R4. Synthetic data only. Customer names, account numbers, card numbers, addresses and transactions are generated by a seeded generator in a shared fixtures package. Card numbers must fail the Luhn check. Account numbers must carry a fictional routing number in the test range. Add a DATA_CLASSIFICATION.md to every repository stating this.

R5. No real bank names or products anywhere, including in comments, commit messages, fixtures, documentation and test data. Grep for them before every commit. The forbidden list is in section 4.

R6. Commit per phase, on a branch per phase, then merge to develop and tag. Write the commit history as if many engineers wrote it over years. Section 9 explains how.

R7. Keep a BUILD_LOG.md at the workspace root. Every substitution, every workaround, every place you were unsure, every command that took more than a minute, and every decision you made on Rhys's behalf goes in it, with the date and the phase.

R8. Ask Rhys only at the decision points marked ASK in section 10. Everything else, decide, record in BUILD_LOG.md, and continue.

R9. Nothing in this estate is production software. Do not add real secrets. Placeholder secrets are fine and should look like placeholders in a way a secret scanner will not flag, for example the string CHANGEME followed by a purpose.

R10. Prose in READMEs and documentation should read like a bank's internal wiki, not like a tutorial. Reference Jira style ticket keys, team names, release train dates and change advisory board approvals. Section 9 gives the conventions.

## 13. Things not to do
